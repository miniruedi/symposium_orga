\begin{conf-abstract}
{Beyond Stationary Models: A Practical Approach to Mixing Ratios and Travel Time in Alluvial Aquifers}
{Théo Blanc, Friederike Currle, Morgan Peel, Yama Tomonaga, Daniel Hunkeler, Oliver Schilling, Rolf Kipfer, Philip Brunner}
{University of Neuchâtel / Eawag / University of Basel}
{Understanding travel times and mixing dynamics in alluvial aquifers is essential for the effective management of riverbank filtration systems, especially under non-stationary conditions caused by restoration works or high-discharge events. Tracer studies are widely used to characterize such systems, but the resulting data are often interpreted using either overly simple stationary models that fail to capture dynamic behavior, or complex numerical simulations that are computationally intensive.
In this study, we present a streamlined modeling framework using gas tracers to characterize surface water–groundwater (SW–GW) mixing and groundwater age in dynamic aquifer conditions. The framework integrates three components: (1) a binary mixing model, (2) an excess air model, and (3) a radon ingrowth model. Together, these tools allow us to resolve short-term variability in a highly dynamic river–aquifer system.

We demonstrate this approach using data collected from an alluvial plain in Bern, Switzerland, where drinking water is abstracted. During the monitoring period, restoration works were carried out in the nearby river. We combined continuous gas monitoring with miniRUEDI and Rad-7 at a groundwater well to track variations in mixing processes and apparent groundwater age over several months.
This modular and low-complexity approach is transferable to other sites where understanding mixing and travel times is critical, such as drinking water abstraction zones or managed aquifer recharge systems.
}
