\begin{conf-abstract}
{NOBLE GAS CONCENTRATIONS IN A BRAZILIAN SEMIARID AQUIFER}
{Natália de Souza Arruda\textsuperscript{1,2}, Hannah Eckert\textsuperscript{2}, Bertram Graf von Reventlow\textsuperscript{2}, Edith
Engelhardt\textsuperscript{2}, Jürgen Sültenfuß\textsuperscript{3}, Didier Gastmans\textsuperscript{1}, Werner Aeschbach\textsuperscript{2}}
{(1) São Paulo State University (UNESP), Environmental Studies Center, Brazil\\
(2) Heidelberg University, Institute of Environmental Physics Heidelberg, Germany\\
(3) Bremen University, Institute of Environmental Physics, Germany}
{The limited recharge rate in confined and deep aquifers in semiarid regions constitutes an
important setting for paleoclimatic studies. The Açu Aquifer is the main aquifer system
responsible for the water supply in the Potiguar Sedimentary Basin, located in the equatorial
margin of South America. Preliminarily, three hydrochemical groups were identified in the
study area, enabling the distinction between recharge zones and confined zones at greater
depths. 14C was used as a qualitative age tracer, and the results showed convergence with
this configuration, presenting more enriched pMC values in regions close to the recharge
areas. At a preliminary stage, it was possible to identify long flow paths and a restricted
recharge zone in the southernmost part of the study area. Initial interpretations of noble
gas (NG) concentrations indicated the distinction of two groups: the first characterized by
high excess air and low NG temperatures; and the second by low excess air and high NG
temperatures. This variation is consistent with patterns generally observed in groundwater
from semiarid regions, where records indicate wetter periods with higher recharge rates.
The main challenge of this study, however, lies in interpreting paleotemperatures in regions
influenced by mantle contributions, as indicated by the NG concentrations and the 3He/ 4He ratio. In addition, the horst and graben structures formed during the basin’s opening, as well as fault systems reactivated after sediment deposition, may have
contributed to the migration of formation waters, oil, and gas. Despite these complexities,a conceptual model of hydrogeochemical flow was developed, which will serve as a basis for more detailed analyses in subsequent stages.

Key words: Brazilian Semiarid Region; Sedimentary Aquifer; Excess Air; Paleotemperature;
Groundwater Flow.}
\end{conf-abstract}
